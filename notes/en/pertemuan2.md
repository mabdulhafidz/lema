# Lecture 2 Notes: History of the Indonesian Language

These notes are taken from the lecturer's explanation in the second lecture.

## 1. Origin of the Name "Indonesia"
The first person to propose the name "Indonesia" was James Richardson Logan.
The name "Indonesia" originates from the combination of islands.
The term comes from the words *Indos* and *Nesos*, proposed by J.R. Logan.

## 2. Definition

The history of the Indonesian language is the journey of the language's development from its earliest period until it became the national language officially used in Indonesia. It covers the processes, events, figures, and factors that influenced its development.

Source: Gorys Keraf

## 3. Objectives of Studying the History of the Indonesian Language
To know the origins of the development of the Indonesian language.
To understand the role of the Indonesian language in the nation's struggle.
To foster a sense of love and pride for the Indonesian language.
To make the Indonesian language a means of national unity and an effective tool of communication.

## 4. Indonesian as an International Language

Indonesian was recognized as an international language on 20 November 2023.

## 5. Characteristics of the History of the Indonesian Language
Related to time (chronological).
Describes a process of change and development.
Involves figures, events, and social-cultural, political, and technological factors.
Factual and objective in nature.
There is continuity between the past, the present, and the future.

## 6. Periods in the Development of the Indonesian Language
Early Period (before the 20th century): Old Malay, Classical Malay, and foreign influences (Arabic, Sanskrit, Portuguese, Dutch).
Colonial Period (19th century – early 20th century): The emergence of Malay as the language of instruction in education, religion, and trade.
National Movement Period (1900–1945): Indonesian began to be used in movement organizations and the mass media.
Independence Period (1945–present): Indonesian was established as the state language and official language, then refined through the Enhanced Spelling System (*Ejaan Yang Disempurnakan*, EYD). This has become EYD Fifth Edition.
Codification Era: Collection, regulation, and standardization of the Indonesian language up to 1972.
Internationalization and Digital Era: Indonesian was planned to become an international language in 2009.

## Additional notes on the periods:

The spelling system used before EYD: the Charles van Ophuijsen Spelling System.
The First Youth Congress and the Second Youth Congress were both held. The Youth Congress was a gathering of young people from various regional organizations in Indonesia that discussed national unity, and it was from the second congress that the Youth Pledge (*Sumpah Pemuda*) was born.

Sources: Chaer, Abdul. 2015. *Linguistik Umum*. Jakarta: Rineka Cipta; and Kemdikbud. 2017. *Sejarah Bahasa Indonesia*.

## 7. Adaptation, Adoption, and Translation

The Indonesian language has developed through these three means: adapting, adopting, and translating words from other languages.

## 8. Key Figures
King Balaputera Dewa – initiator of the use of Malay as a language of instruction.
Muhammad Yamin – pioneer of Indonesian as the national language.
Soewandi – formulator of terminology and grammar (the Soewandi Spelling System / Republican Spelling System, 1947).
Chairil Anwar – elevated the Indonesian language into the realm of modern literature.
Raden Alit Prawatasari – a hero from Cianjur.

Source: Dalman. 2018. *Sejarah Bahasa Indonesia*. Jakarta: Rajawali Pers.

## 9. The Relationship Between Indonesian and Malay

Malay was once the language used throughout the Archipelago (Nusantara), and the Indonesian language has its roots in Malay.

## 10. Formula for the Constituent Elements of the Indonesian Language

The Indonesian language is formed from:

BI = BM + BD + BA

BI: *Bahasa Indonesia* (Indonesian Language)
BM: *Bahasa Melayu* (Malay Language)
BD: *Bahasa Daerah* (Regional Languages)
BA: *Bahasa Asing* (Foreign Languages)

## 11. Other Terms
Ethnicity (*etnis*): ethnic group.
