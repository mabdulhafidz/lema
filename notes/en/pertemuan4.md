# Lecture 4: Functions, Status, and Registers of Language

These notes are taken from the lecturer's explanation in the fourth lecture.

## 1. Common Language Errors

- Use of non-standard language.
- Sentences that are too long and ineffective.
- Use of ambiguous words.
- Lack of cohesion between paragraphs.

## 2. Material: Functions, Status, and Registers of Language

### 2.1 Definition of Language Function

Language function is the value of language use, that is, the usefulness of language in daily life in formal, semi-formal, and informal settings.

**Functions of language:**

1. A tool for communication.
2. A tool for self-expression.
3. A tool for integration and adaptation.
4. A tool for social control.
5. A tool for thinking and learning.
6. A tool for recognizing one's own abilities.
7. A tool for building and developing a profession.
8. A tool for creativity.

### 2.2 Status of Language

The status (*kedudukan*) of a language is its relative standing compared with other languages in a given region.

**Status of Indonesian as the national language:**

1. A symbol of national pride.
2. A symbol of national identity.
3. A tool for uniting the various ethnic groups.

**Status of Indonesian as the state language:**

1. The official language of the state, covering:
   - The language of state affairs.
   - State speeches.
   - Official correspondence.
2. The language for the development of culture and of science and technology.
3. The language of instruction in education, from primary school through higher education.
4. The language of communication at the national level.

### 2.3 Definition of Language Register

A language register (*ragam bahasa*) is a variation in language use by an individual or a group of people.

**Causes of the emergence of registers:**

- Differences in speakers.
- Differences in medium.
- Differences in situation.
- Differences in field.

Geographic location also usually affects differences in pronunciation (accent or dialect).

## 3. The Register of Academic Writing

*(For students of the Faculty of Engineering)*

The academic register is the variety of language used in writing scholarly works, characterized as formal, direct, objective, and systematic.

**Example of the academic register:**

> *"Penelitian ini bertujuan untuk mengetahui pengaruh pola belajar terhadap hasil belajar siswa."*
> ("This study aims to determine the effect of study patterns on students' learning outcomes.")

That sentence uses formal, clear language that goes straight to the point.

**Linguistic rules for academic writing:**

- Use effective sentences.
- Pay attention to language structure in academic work.
- Sentences should not be too long.

**Example of an ambiguous vs. an effective sentence:**

- Ambiguous: *"Dokter baru berangkat tadi pagi."* (can mean "the new doctor left this morning" or "the doctor just left this morning")
- Effective: *"Dokter berangkat tadi pagi."* ("The doctor left this morning." — the meaning is clear and allows no double interpretation)

## 4. Assignments

### 4.1 Lecture Summary

This lecture discussed three main topics: the functions, status, and registers of language. Language has many functions in daily life, ranging from a tool for communication to a tool for creativity. The Indonesian language itself holds a dual status: as the national language (a symbol of national pride and identity, and a unifier of Indonesia's ethnic groups) and as the state language (the official language of the state, the language of instruction in education, and the language for developing culture and science and technology).

In addition, language has registers that arise from differences in speakers, media, situations, and fields of use. One register that is important for students to learn, especially in the Faculty of Engineering, is the academic register: one that is formal and direct, and avoids ambiguous or overly long sentences. Awareness of the functions, status, and registers of language is important so that we can choose the right language for the situation, especially when writing academic work.

Below is a sample seminar opening script following the presentation structure taught (opening greeting, forms of address, expression of gratitude, and introduction of the topic). The parts in square brackets can be adjusted to the actual event.

> Assalamu'alaikum warahmatullahi wabarakatuh, good morning, and greetings of peace to us all.
>
> To the honorable Mr./Mrs. [name/title of the Chair], and to the honorable ladies and gentlemen and all those present,
>
> First of all, let us offer praise and thanks to God Almighty, for by His grace and blessing we are able to gather here in good health to attend today's seminar.
>
> Allow me to open this seminar on the topic of "[seminar topic]" on this joyful occasion. May this seminar bring benefit and broaden the knowledge of us all.
>
> With the words bismillahirrahmanirrahim, I hereby declare the seminar on the topic of "[seminar topic]" officially open.
>
> Wassalamu'alaikum warahmatullahi wabarakatuh.

**Note:** If there is more than one speaker, mention only the seminar topic. If there is only one speaker, the title or topic can be stated directly without further explanation.
