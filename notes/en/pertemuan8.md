# Meeting 8: Scientific Writing

These notes are based on my lecturer's explanation in the eighth meeting (14 November 2026).

## 1. Definition of Scientific Writing

A scientific work is writing that presents the results of thinking, observation, review, or research in a particular field. It is not based on opinion or feelings.

## 2. Types of Scientific Writing

1. Paper (with conclusions and suggestions)
2. Working paper
3. Laboratory report
4. Article
5. Journal paper
6. Field internship report
7. Undergraduate thesis (for a bachelor's degree)
8. Master's thesis (for a master's degree)
9. Dissertation (for a doctoral degree)

## 3. Structure of Scientific Writing

1. **Title page**
2. **Abstract**: a summary or overview, maximum 250 words (it must not exceed 250 words).
3. **Introduction**, which contains:
   - Background of the problem
   - Problem statement
   - Research objectives
   - Research benefits
4. **Theoretical framework**: the theories that underlie the research.
5. **Research hypothesis**: a temporary answer to the proposed research question.
6. **Research method**, which contains:
   - Type of research
   - Definition of concepts and operational definition of variables
   - Population and research sample
   - Type and source of data
   - Data collection techniques
   - Data analysis or testing techniques

   **Qualitative** research focuses more on describing, while **quantitative** research is more concerned with numbers.
7. **Definition of concepts**
8. **Discussion of the research**
9. **Conclusion**: summary and suggestions.

## 4. Language Rules for Scientific Writing

1. **Use impersonal words**: not based on the researcher's feelings; it must be objective and factual.
2. **Use passive sentences.**
3. **Use reproductive language**: language that lets readers understand the author's intent in the same way the author does.
4. **Use denotative language**: words used with their literal meaning.
5. **Use standard words**: words in their official, standard form.

## 5. The Scientific Process (John Dewey)

1. Identify and formulate the problem (there are 3 questions).
2. Build the framework of thinking.
3. Formulate a hypothesis.
4. Test the hypothesis (conduct a review).
5. Draw conclusions (written as bullet points).

## 6. Steps in Writing a Scientific Work

1. Determine the research theme or topic.
2. Create an outline or research framework.

## 7. Practice Questions

### 1. What do you know about scientific writing?

Scientific writing is writing that presents the results of thinking, observation, review, or research in a particular field. Its content is not based on the author's opinions or feelings, but is prepared objectively and systematically, using standard language.

### 2. What are the characteristics of scientific writing?

- Objective and factual, not based on opinion or feelings.
- Uses impersonal words.
- Uses passive sentences.
- Uses reproductive language, so readers understand the author's intent in the same way.
- Uses denotative language, meaning words with their literal meaning.
- Uses standard words.
- Has a systematic structure, from the title page and abstract to the introduction and conclusion.
- Follows the scientific process, from formulating the problem to drawing conclusions.

### 3. What kinds of scientific writing do you commonly write?

As a student, the scientific works I commonly write are papers, laboratory reports, articles, and field internship reports. At the end of a bachelor's program, students also write an undergraduate thesis.

### 4. Explain the structure of scientific writing!

1. **Title page**: contains the title and identity of the work.
2. **Abstract**: a summary or overview of the work, maximum 250 words.
3. **Introduction**: contains the background of the problem, problem statement, research objectives, and research benefits.
4. **Theoretical framework**: the theories that underlie the research.
5. **Research hypothesis**: a temporary answer to the research question.
6. **Research method**: contains the type of research, definition of concepts and operational definition of variables, population and sample, type and source of data, data collection techniques, and data analysis or testing techniques. Qualitative research focuses on describing, while quantitative research is more concerned with numbers.
7. **Definition of concepts**: an explanation of the concepts used.
8. **Discussion of the research**: a description of the research results.
9. **Conclusion**: contains the summary and suggestions.

### 5. What is the scientific procedure?

According to John Dewey, the scientific procedure consists of five stages:

1. Identify and formulate the problem.
2. Build the framework of thinking.
3. Formulate a hypothesis.
4. Test the hypothesis (conduct a review).
5. Draw conclusions, written as bullet points.
