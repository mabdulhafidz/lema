# Lecture 3: Phonemes, Morphemes, Words, Terms, and Diction

These notes are taken from the lecturer's explanation in the third lecture.

## 1. Material and Objectives

This lecture covers phonemes, morphemes, words, terms, and diction. The aim is for us to be conscious when writing or pronouncing words, and to understand the concept of the phoneme.

## 2. Linguistic Units (Language Structure)

A **linguist** is the term for a language expert. Language standardization takes place in the world of education.

### 2.1 Phonology

Phonology is the study of speech sounds.

- **Phoneme**: the smallest unit of sound that distinguishes meaning.
- Sounds consist of **vowels** and **consonants**.

### 2.2 Morphology

Morphology is the study of the process of word formation.

- **Morpheme**: the smallest grammatical unit; the building block of words.
- Morphemes are divided into:
  - **Free morphemes**
  - **Bound morphemes**

### 2.3 Words

A word is a unit of language that carries meaning and whose form is no longer bound.

**Word classes:**

- Nouns (*nomina*)
- Verbs (*verba*)
- Adjectives (*adjektiva*)
- Pronouns (*pronomina*)

**Word-formation processes:**

- Affixation (adding affixes)
- Reduplication (repeating a word)

## 3. Diction

Diction means using the right choice of words, paying attention to:

- Accuracy
- Appropriateness
- Variety
- Sentence effectiveness
- Denotative and connotative meaning — denotative is the literal meaning.

## 4. Terms

- A term is the vocabulary of a particular field.
- Its meaning is definite (fixed) and monosemous (has a single meaning).
- It is standardized in nature and used for specific purposes.
- There is a difference between general vocabulary and specialized vocabulary.

**Example of the prefix "ber-" (meaning "to wear/use"):**

> *Mahasiswa disarankan untuk berjaket.* (Students are advised to wear a jacket.)

"Article" can also be discussed as a term in the context of types of written work.

## 5. Correlative Conjunctions

Correlative conjunctions are paired connecting words used to link two equivalent elements of a sentence.

Examples of pairs:

- *baik ... maupun ...* (both ... and ...)
- *tidak hanya ... tetapi juga ...* (not only ... but also ...)
- *entah ... entah ...* (whether ... or ...)
- *bukan hanya ... melainkan juga ...* (not only ... but also ...)

## 6. Verbs (*Verba*)

**Examples of verbs:** *belajar* (to study), *pergi* (to go), *bersepeda* (to cycle).

**Derived verbs:**

- Affixed verbs, e.g. *menyanyi* (to sing).

**The relationship between verbs and nouns in a sentence (voice/diathesis):**

- Active
- Passive / anti-active / ergative
- Antipassive

**Characteristics of verbs:**

- Can take a time aspect (*akan* "will", *sedang* "is -ing", *telah* "has").
- Can be negated with the word *tidak* ("not"), e.g. *tidak pergi* (did not go).
- Can be combined in the pattern verb + *dengan* ("with") + noun.
- Cannot be combined with *sangat* ("very"), *paling* ("most"), or *agak* ("rather").

## 7. Adjectives (*Adjektiva*)

An adjective is a word used to describe or attribute a quality to a noun. Adjectives provide additional information about the shape, color, size, or other characteristics of the thing in question.

## 8. Adverbs (*Adverbia*)

Adverbs do not only indicate place; they can also indicate time.

## 9. Rules for Writing "di-"

*Di* can be a **prefix** (written joined to the word) or a **preposition** (written separately). How to tell them apart:

- If a word with the prefix *di-* can be changed into a word with the prefix *me-* (the active form), then *di* is a **prefix** and is written **joined** to its base word.
  Example: *dipeluk* (hugged) → can be changed to *memeluk* (to hug), so it is written as one word: *dipeluk*.
- If the word after *di* is a noun or a place and cannot be changed into a *me-* form, then *di* is a **preposition** and is written **separately**.
  Example: *di rumah* (at home), *di sekolah* (at school).

## 10. Assignments

### 10.1 Reading Report: Diction in Formal and Informal Communication

**Introduction**

Diction is the choice of words a person uses to convey ideas, whether spoken or written. The accuracy of diction determines whether the message can be clearly understood by the listener or reader, and at the same time shows how well the language fits the communicative situation taking place.

**Discussion**

*Diction in formal communication*

Formal communication is used in official situations, such as lectures, meetings, official correspondence, or academic writing. The diction used must be standard, in accordance with the KBBI (the official Indonesian dictionary) and PUEBI (the general spelling guidelines), and must avoid abbreviations or everyday conversational language.

- Formal spoken: presenting a class assignment, a speech, or a seminar discussion. Example: "Based on the data we have collected, it can be concluded that ..."
- Formal written: reports, theses, official letters. Example: "In connection with this matter, we intend to submit ..."

*Diction in informal communication*

Informal communication is used in relaxed and familiar situations, for example with friends or family. Its diction is looser and may include abbreviations, slang, or regional dialect.

- Informal spoken: chatting with friends. Example: *"Udah makan belum? Nanti jadi kumpul, kan?"* ("Have you eaten yet? We're still meeting up later, right?")
- Informal written: text messages or social media chats. Example: *"btw besok jadi gak nih ketemuan?"* ("btw are we still meeting tomorrow or not?")

*Comparison*

The main difference between formal and informal diction lies in the standardness of the words, sentence structure, and level of familiarity. Formal communication demands care in choosing words because it relates to credibility and clarity of the message, whereas informal communication prioritizes closeness and practicality.

**Conclusion**

The right choice of diction needs to be adjusted to the context, the interlocutor, and the communication medium used. In formal situations, standard diction maintains clarity and politeness, while in informal situations, more relaxed diction helps maintain familiarity. This awareness in choosing diction is the core lesson of this lecture.

### 10.2 20 Terms in the Field of Informatics

1. **Algorithm** – logical and systematic steps for solving a problem.
2. **Variable** – a storage location for a value that can change within a program.
3. **Function** – a block of code designed to perform a specific task that can be called repeatedly.
4. **Database** – an organized collection of data that can be accessed systematically.
5. **Server** – a computer or system that provides services or data to other computers (clients).
6. **Client** – a device or program that requests services from a server.
7. **API (Application Programming Interface)** – an interface that allows two applications to communicate with each other.
8. **Framework** – a structure containing ready-to-use code to speed up software development.
9. **Debugging** – the process of finding and fixing errors (bugs) in program code.
10. **Compiler** – a program that translates source code into machine language all at once before it is run.
11. **Interpreter** – a program that translates and executes source code line by line.
12. **Query** – a command to retrieve, add, modify, or delete data in a database.
13. **Array** – a data structure that stores a collection of values of the same data type in a single variable.
14. **Looping** – a control structure for repeating a block of code several times.
15. **Boolean** – a data type that has only two values: true or false.
16. **Bug** – an error or defect in a program that causes results that do not match expectations.
17. **Encryption** – the process of converting data into coded form so that it cannot be read by unauthorized parties.
18. **Protocol** – a standard set of rules governing how devices or systems communicate with each other, e.g. HTTP or TCP/IP.
19. **Interface** – the part of a system that serves as the point of interaction between the user and the program.
20. **Repository** – a storage location for program code along with its change history, e.g. on GitHub.
