# Lecture 6: Paragraphs

These notes are taken from the lecturer's explanation in the sixth lecture.

## 1. Definition of a Paragraph

A paragraph is a combination of several sentences that are interrelated and support one another to express a single main idea or central thought. The sentences that build a paragraph must have cohesion and coherence.

- **Cohesion**: unity of thought.
- **Coherence**: the sentences must be unified or connected to one another.

## 2. Ways to Build Cohesion and Coherence

1. Repetition.
2. Transition.
3. Orderly arrangement of ideas.
4. Conjunctions (connecting words).
5. Substitution (pronouns) — for example, replacing a person's name with the pronoun "he/she" (*dia*).
6. Parallelism.

**Example of a cohesive and coherent paragraph:**

> Education is a process for building a generation of quality in every aspect of life. Among other things, it builds in learners a character grounded in faith, devotion, morality, and sound ethics. The process takes place not only in formal institutions, but also at home and in the community.

**Example of a paragraph that is neither cohesive nor coherent:**

> Education is a process for building a generation of quality in every aspect of life. Society becomes smart and happy. The process takes place not only in formal institutions, but also at home and in the community.

## 3. Types of Paragraphs by Nature and Purpose

1. **Opening paragraph**
2. **Linking/body paragraph**
3. **Closing paragraph**

### 3.1 Opening Paragraph

The initial part of a piece of writing, which plays an important role in attracting attention and stimulating the reader's curiosity, as well as providing a general overview.

### 3.2 Linking/Body Paragraph

Essentially the main content that the writer wants to convey to the reader. This paragraph bridges or connects one idea/topic with the next, and helps keep the flow of the writing cohesive and coherent so that the reader can follow it.

### 3.3 Closing Paragraph

Leans more toward a conclusion or final statement. This last part of a piece of writing serves to summarize or reaffirm the main points discussed earlier. In an essay or article, the closing paragraph often serves to leave a strong final impression on the reader.

### 3.4 Examples of the Three Types of Paragraph

**Example of an opening paragraph:**

> According to Sheikh Muhammad Mahmud in his book *Awa'idush Shalah*, prayer (*shalat*) is an act of worship that connects human beings with their Lord. This shows the lofty status contained within prayer. Moreover, prayer is also the highest act of worship in Islam.

**Example of a linking paragraph:**

> The ritual of prayer makes no distinction between the rich and the poor, the highly educated or otherwise. All people set aside the attributes, titles, and social status they carry. They hold the same standing in the sight of God, namely as servants.

**Example of a closing paragraph:**

> It cannot be denied that prayer is a true manifestation of faith. By performing prayer, a person gains a number of benefits. Among the benefits of prayer is that it makes a person's face radiant.

## 4. Ways to Begin a Paragraph

1. Quoting the opinion of an expert.
2. Stating something universal (general).
3. Writing a definition.
4. Posing a rhetorical question (a question raised and answered by the writer themselves).
5. Presenting an interesting fact.

## 5. Requirements of a Good Paragraph

1. Has one main idea or central thought that is the focus of discussion.
2. Has supporting sentences that back up that main idea.
3. Has unity and cohesion between sentences.
4. Has a closing sentence that states a conclusion.

## 6. Position of the Main Idea in a Paragraph

- **Deductive**: the main idea is at the beginning of the paragraph.
- Some are also located in the middle of the paragraph.
- **Inductive**: the main idea is at the end of the paragraph.
- **Mixed**: the main idea is at both the beginning and the end of the paragraph.

In descriptive and narrative paragraphs, the main idea is usually spread across the whole paragraph rather than located in any particular sentence.

## 7. Paragraph Development

In general, paragraph development consists of:

1. Description
2. Exposition
3. Persuasion
4. Argumentation
5. Narration

According to some experts, paragraph development is grouped into only 3 main types: **description, persuasion, and narration**.

- **Description**: usually writing that depicts something. Example: describing the situation in Jakarta.
- **Argumentation**: leans more toward patterns of thinking (reasoning).
- **Exposition**: leans more toward knowledge, using plain language. Exposition can develop into persuasion if it contains an appeal or call to action.
- **Narration**: encompasses everything; usually a story told in sequence.

## 8. Assignment 6

**Instructions:** Write a discourse on the topic of the benefits of computers, consisting of 3 paragraphs or 300 words, made up of an opening paragraph, a body paragraph, and a closing paragraph.

### The Benefits of Computers for Human Life

**Opening Paragraph**

The computer has become one of the most influential tools in modern human life. Since it was first invented, the computer has continued to evolve from a device used only for calculation into a multifunctional tool that supports nearly all of humanity's daily activities. Its presence makes a wide range of work easier in many fields, from education and the workplace to health and communication. In fact, there is hardly any area of life today untouched by computer technology. It is therefore important for us to understand the various benefits of computers in everyday life.

**Body Paragraph**

In the field of education, computers make the teaching and learning process easier by enabling broad and rapid access to information through the internet, whether for finding references or taking part in online learning. In the workplace, computers help complete various administrative tasks, data processing, and calculations faster and more accurately than manual methods. Computers also play an important role in health care, for example in storing patients' medical records digitally and supporting the diagnostic process with the help of specialized software. In addition, computers make it easy for people to communicate over long distances through various messaging and video-calling applications, so that distance is no longer a major barrier to staying connected. With all these conveniences, computers contribute greatly to increasing human productivity in almost every sector of life.

**Closing Paragraph**

From the explanation above, it can be concluded that computers provide many benefits for human life, whether in education, work, health, or communication. These benefits will most likely continue to grow alongside future technological developments, for example through artificial intelligence and automation. Nevertheless, computer use also needs to be balanced with discipline so that it does not cause negative effects such as addiction or excessive dependence. For this reason, wise and responsible use of computers must continue to be promoted so that its benefits can be felt to the fullest by everyone.
